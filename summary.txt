World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi agreed a prisoner swap of 1,000 to 1,500 each, Kushner said, but no ceasefire or territory deal (Reuters). Kushner and Witkoff fly to Moscow. Russia hit six power substations; an Odesa market strike killed three.

At COP31, UN chief Antonio Guterres urged windfall-profit taxes, noting the nine biggest firms earned $95 billion in a quarter, the Fund $600 million (UN News). He proposed a "climate justice compact" and warned of 2.5-plus degrees of warming even if plans are kept.

The US charged three men over Iran-backed plots, including RAF Fairford; Treasury chief Bessent was moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Iran's negotiator said Hormuz, shut over 250 days, "will never return" to its pre-war state (Al Jazeera).

In Spain, four million workers struck for the first time in 14 years over housing, ahead of a 29 November election (Reuters). Rent averages 1,176 euros a month — 98.7 percent of a young worker's pay (Youth Council).

British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer at a NATO corps near Gloucester for spying for Russia; the Kremlin denied any link (Reuters).

Russia's ambassador to the US, Alexander Darchiev, died suddenly in Moscow aged 66 (Reuters). A spokeswoman said he died of a chronic illness; envoy since March 2025.

Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to an Ebola-free declaration on 23 December (Reuters). The outbreak caused nine cases, seven recoveries, two deaths.

Israel's new parliament was sworn in with no government, Netanyahu's bloc on 53 of 120 seats (AP). Israeli strikes killed at least eight in Gaza; floods swamped 70,000 tents (Al Jazeera/CARE).

RSF drones killed nine displaced civilians in Sudan's Blue Nile state heading for shelters in Damazin (Xinhua).

Nvidia's board met late Wednesday on ousting CEO Jensen Huang over a China spy scandal; shares slid over 8% after hours (Reuters).
